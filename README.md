# Community-Perspectives-on-Environmental-Challenges-and-Information-Needs-in-Hunza
Case study: Community research and participatory design for an environmental information service in Hunza
Concept prototype. All prototype content is sample content. It contains no real alerts, data, phone numbers or organisations.

Research question

How can community research and participatory design inform the development of a trustworthy, accessible environmental information service that addresses the information needs of communities in Hunza?

What I did
Step	Detail
Community survey	Google Forms, 2–15 Aug 2026. 44 submissions, 43 consenting respondents analysed.
Farmer interviews	Interview-style survey, 24 Aug–25 Oct 2026. 5 farmers from 5 villages.
Prototype	5-screen mobile prototype built in Google Stitch (Home, Safety, Farming, Sources, Community).
Feedback sessions	5 participants (P01–P05), 5 tasks each, plus interview questions.

The two surveys are not pooled. They differ in size, question type and respondents.

Key findings
Floods and glacier hazards are the main concerns. Flooding was chosen by 90.7% and glacier-related issues by 86.0% of community respondents. Three of 5 farmers described floods or GLOF events.
Preparedness and early warning come first. Emergency preparedness was chosen by 83.7% and flood and landslide awareness by 76.7%. About half of open answers asked for warnings or training.
Trust is moderate, and information can arrive late. 88.4% use social media, but mean trust is 3.72/5. Two of 5 farmers said information does not reach them on time.
Language matters. Urdu (67.4%), English (65.1%) and Wakhi (65.1%) were each wanted by about two thirds. Three of 5 farmers asked for local-language information.
People want a say. 95.3% said community members should help design solutions, and all 5 farmers rely on community, family or farmer networks. Four of 5 farmers described water-related problems.
How the findings shaped the design
Finding	Design decision
Floods, GLOFs and preparedness	Safety screen with alert card and Before / During / After guide
Trust and late information	Source, date and source type on every item
Water problems	Farming screen with sample water channel and irrigation cards
Language	Urdu version of Home, language options on Community
Community involvement	Feedback form, "Suggest a source", "Suggest a change to this design"
Channel unknown	Alert delivery options left open and unchecked for testing

Possible role of AI, and conditions

The research does not yet show a need for AI, and the prototype has no AI feature. If AI were added later, for example to summarise or sort source material for human review, it should come only after a need is confirmed, sources are reliable, and the people who would use it have tested it. Risks include errors in translation or summaries, over-reliance on automated output, and replacing trusted community knowledge.

Limitations and open questions
Small, non-random samples. The community sample is young (76.7% aged 18–30), mostly non-farming (4 of 43 farmers) and concentrated in Gulmit and Gojal (60.5%).
Farmer findings rest on 5 interviews and are exploratory.
Themes were coded by one analyst.
Phone ownership, connectivity, literacy and preferred channels were not measured.
Who would manage and verify information, and how, is not yet designed.
Feedback comes from 5 participants. It shows design problems, not how common they are.
Tourism and development pressure appeared in some open answers and is out of scope for now.
Privacy

No personal data is included. Respondents and participants appear by ID only. Raw survey files are not published.
